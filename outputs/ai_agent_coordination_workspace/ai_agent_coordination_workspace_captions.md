## Topic: The bottleneck moved to coordination, not intelligence

---

## LinkedIn

AI models can already do the work. The real bottleneck on your team just moved somewhere else: coordination.

Most teams building with AI agents are still stuck in the same pattern. One person, one agent, one private chat window. Every handoff needs a human to copy the output somewhere else, explain what just happened, and restart the conversation. Nobody enjoys being the middleware between two machines.

The fix isn't a smarter model. It's a shared place for people and agents to actually work together, the same way a team channel replaced a hundred 1:1 emails.

What that actually requires:
→ A channel every human and agent can see, not a private prompt history
→ Every agent gets its own signed identity, so nothing borrows a human's credentials
→ Authorization that's scoped and revocable, not a shared login everyone forgets to rotate
→ A visible record of what was tried and rejected, not just the final diff
→ Room for cheap, fast agents to work in parallel under one person's direction

None of this is exotic. It's the same discipline that made human teams work well, applied to a room where some of the teammates happen to be models.

If your team is scaling up agent usage this year, the coordination layer is where you'll actually get stuck, not the model choice.
