## LinkedIn

Most AML alerts are wrong.

Rule-based transaction monitoring flags 90 to 95% false positives. Compliance teams spend most of their week clearing customers who did nothing wrong, while the industry still racks up over $36B in AML fines in a decade, almost all from what the system missed, not what it wrongly flagged.

Raising the threshold doesn't fix this. It just trades one failure for another: fewer alerts, more real launderers slipping through.

AI agents break that trade-off differently. Instead of checking whether a single transaction crossed a line, they score every alert against a customer's own history, their peer group, and the surrounding transaction network, then write down the reasoning for a human to review.

What banks running this are reporting:
→ Far fewer false positives
→ 2 to 4x more genuine detections, not fewer
→ A written, auditable rationale on every decision
→ Regulators increasingly expect this, as long as it's explainable

The right way to start isn't a full rollout. Run agents in shadow mode alongside your existing system for 3 to 6 months, prove detection holds, then automate the lowest risk tier first.

The goal was never a smaller alert queue. It's a better sorted one.
