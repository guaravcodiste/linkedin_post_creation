## LinkedIn

Your procurement committee approved the budget. Your Head of AI shortlisted three vendors. Now you need to know which one stays accountable six months after the contract closes.

The wrong AI agent development company does not just delay your roadmap. It hands you a fragile prototype you cannot take to production without rebuilding the core architecture.

Most enterprise buyers discover this gap only after signing.

Enterprise buyers who skipped production diligence reported average rebuild timelines of seven to nine months. The sunk cost is not just the vendor fee. It is the internal credibility you spent building stakeholder buy-in for a system that cannot ship.

What actually separates production-ready firms from demo shops:
- They architect for failure first: explicit fallback paths, escalation triggers, observable logging from day one
- They separate orchestration from task execution: model is swappable without rewriting the workflow graph
- They treat post-launch support as a named engagement, not a helpdesk queue
- Compliance is integrated into the architecture from day one, not added in a final legal review

Five questions every vendor must answer before you sign:
1. Show me a production deployment with observable metrics. Not your demo environment.
2. How do you handle agent failure mid-workflow? Name the fallback strategy.
3. Who owns the IP and codebase after delivery?
4. How do you handle compliance for regulated verticals from day one?
5. Who specifically is on post-launch support, and what is their SLA?

Require written answers. Verbal commitments do not hold up post-contract.
