## LinkedIn

Your AI proof of concept is in its seventh month.

The model works. The demo impressed the board. The pilot ran on synthetic data with three internal users.

Now your CTO is asking what it takes to move to production, where the agent touches live customer data, writes to production databases, and operates under the regulatory controls that govern your business.

The answer is not a larger budget. It is a governance framework your POC was never designed to produce.

60% of enterprise AI initiatives that complete a successful POC do not reach production within 18 months. The technology works. The governance does not.

Here is what separates a POC from a production deployment:

-> Access control model: document exactly what systems the agent can read, write, and under what conditions
-> Change management: version-controlled configuration with an approval process for every behavior change
-> Audit trail architecture: immutable log of every agent action before output propagates downstream
-> Failure mode documentation: defined behavior for every failure state, not just the happy path
-> Human override protocol: clear escalation path when agent confidence falls below threshold

These are not compliance checkboxes. They are the engineering spec for production readiness. Teams that treat governance as documentation-after-the-fact spend months in remediation. Teams that treat it as the architecture input build it correctly once.
