A compliance team at a Series B payments company processes 400 new account applications per day. Manual document review occupies three staff members for eight hours each. The KYC queue backs up over weekends, and the 48-hour onboarding SLA breaks every Friday.

This is a solvable architecture problem.

AI agents automate document extraction, identity verification, risk scoring, and adverse media checks in a single orchestrated workflow. AML continuous monitoring replaces batch cycles with event-driven agent loops that process each transaction in real time.

Production results from a fintech processing $120M in monthly volume:
- KYC onboarding time: 5.2 days to 18 hours
- AML false positive rate: 34% reduction
- Transaction processing: 95% under 800 milliseconds
- SAR preparation: 2 to 4 hours reduced to 15 to 30 minutes, with evidence pre-assembled by the agent

The architecture difference that matters: batch AML cycles allow suspicious transactions to settle before the flag generates. An event-driven agent loop fires before settlement. The SAR arrives on time.

FinCEN updated its guidance in 2024: institutions processing more than $50M in monthly volume should move toward continuous risk monitoring. Periodic review is no longer the baseline expectation.

The non-negotiables for production: human escalation gates for every high-risk case, audit logging that captures every agent decision and tool call, and a log schema that functions as an evidentiary record for examination.
