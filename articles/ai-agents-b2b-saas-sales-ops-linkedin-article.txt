TITLE:
AI Agents for Sales Ops: Fixing the Data Behind the Pipeline

A demo request lands at 6:12 p.m. on a Friday. The routing rule fires by territory, and the record still says Chicago — two years after the company moved its headquarters and buying committee to Austin. The lead drops into the wrong rep's queue. She works it Monday at 9:04 a.m., sixty-three hours later. A competitor called back in four minutes.

Nobody made a mistake. The form worked. The rule fired exactly as written. The rep worked her queue in order. The only thing that failed was a single field that was true when someone typed it and quietly stopped being true.

## Enrichment, Routing, and Forecasting Share One Cause

B2B SaaS sales ops runs on records that decay. HubSpot's Database Decay Simulation puts monthly B2B contact decay at 2.1%, compounding to roughly 22.5% a year. Dun & Bradstreet puts it at 30-40% annually in high-mobility sectors like SaaS, with job titles decaying fastest of any field.

Enrichment decides what a record says. Routing decides where it goes and how fast. Forecast hygiene decides whether the number sent to the board is real. All three read from the same decaying source, and all three fail quietly enough that the postmortem usually blames the reps.

The order matters here too: you can't route on a field you haven't enriched, and you can't forecast a pipeline that was misrouted in the first place.

## Why Quarterly Cleanups Can't Keep Up

The math doesn't hold up, and neither does the usual workaround:

- At 2.1% monthly decay, a database cleaned in January is roughly 6% wrong by April and 12% wrong by July — you're always cleaning a version that no longer exists.
- The MIT and InsideSales study found a five-minute lead response makes qualification 21 times more likely than a thirty-minute one, yet the B2B average still sits at 42-47 hours.
- A 2024 test of 1,000 B2B SaaS companies found 63.5% never responded to an inbound lead at all.
- Only 35% of sales professionals completely trust the accuracy of their CRM data, per Salesforce's State of Sales research.
- Reps spend roughly 30% of the week actually selling, with CRM data entry absorbing much of the rest — asking them to "keep records current" collides with how the week is already spent.

## What Changes When Agents Run Continuously

Instead of a quarterly project, agents re-verify records on a clock and write with a confidence score and a source attached, so a human can audit any decision:

- Enrichment agents score every write and flag conflicts between sources instead of silently resolving them. A single provider typically matches only 30-60% of records, so cascading a second source to fill what the first missed lifts coverage without a bigger contract.
- Routing agents enrich the record before the routing rule fires, then enforce response-time SLAs as a live condition instead of a reported metric.
- Forecast hygiene agents check stage evidence, stale deals, and close-date drift against a deal's own activity history — XANT Labs found 47% of deals missed forecast by more than half, and Gartner reports fewer than 25% of sales organizations forecast above 75% accuracy.

The order matters: enrichment first, in suggest mode, promoted by confidence tier. Routing and forecasting inherit the benefit once the fields underneath them are current.

## Speed Without a Fixed Foundation Backfires

Gartner projects more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, and IDC puts the share of AI pilots that never reach production at 88%. Both point to weak data foundations and governance gaps, not model quality.

Deloitte's 2026 research across 3,235 leaders found only 21% of organizations have a mature governance model for autonomous agents, while 74% plan to expand agentic deployment within two years — intent is running well ahead of controls.

An agent pointed at a CRM that's 30% wrong doesn't fix the 30%. It writes into it faster, with more confidence, and with less friction than the process it replaced. A rep who mistypes a territory creates one bad record; an agent inferring territory from a decayed field creates thousands, each carrying an authoritative-looking timestamp.

Fix enrichment and the routing improvement comes close to free. Fix routing and the forecast starts describing something real. A tool that only fills more fields is a faster version of the problem — one that tells you which fields it doesn't trust, and why, is doing the job.
